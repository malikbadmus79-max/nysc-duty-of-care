Between April 2011 and October 2026, national news reports record 96 incidents of serious harm to National Youth Service Corps members: 151 corps members abducted and 65 killed. Kidnapping is the most common harm.

An independent, pre-registered analysis of NYSC duty of care, built from public documents and news reports, finds:

- NYSC rules score 12 of 36 against the duty-of-care standard of the Global Interagency Security Forum. The June 2026 reform, as announced, raises this to 14. Informed consent, incident reporting and kidnap response score 0 under both.
- Risk concentrates around official travel dates. 52% of exactly dated incidents on journeys to and from camp, and in camp, fell within 7 days of a camp-opening or passing-out date, against 31% of calendar days.
- Risk per corps member deployed differs by state. Akwa Ibom, Rivers, Katsina, the FCT and Bayelsa have the highest rates. States with more kidnapping of the general population tend to have higher rates for corps members, but only moderately, so general insecurity data alone would miss part of the risk.
- Northern incidents received as much news coverage per incident as southern ones.

The reform approved in June 2026 is the moment to write a duty of care into the amended NYSC Act: a safety procedure for journeys between states, risk information before deployment, a confidential incident reporting channel, and a published kidnap response plan.

An audit of NYSC security-related spending from 2018 to 2026 is in progress.

The policy brief, data, code and an interactive dashboard are published with the project. Victim names are not stored, in line with the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023.
