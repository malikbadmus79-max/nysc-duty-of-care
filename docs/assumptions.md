# Assumptions

Log of assumptions made in the analysis, referenced by ID wherever they are used in code, notebooks or reports.

| ID | Assumption | Reason | Used in | Sensitivity |
|----|------------|--------|---------|-------------|
| A01 | An incident is dated by the day the harm began (the day of abduction or attack), not the publication date of the report. Where only the month or year is known, the date is set to the first day of that month or year and `date_precision` records the precision. | News reports often appear one or more days after an event, and some reports give only an approximate date. | `nysc_safety.coding`; `docs/coding_protocol.md`; H2 travel-window test | Incidents with month or year precision are excluded from the H2 test, which needs day-level dates; the count excluded is reported with the result. |
| A02 | The number of corps members exposed in a state in a year equals the number sworn in at that state's orientation camp across all batches and streams of the year. | NYSC does not publish a time series of serving corps members by state; swearing-in figures are reported by state coordinators at each batch. | `nysc_safety.rates`; H3 and H4 | Excludes corps members redeployed into or out of a state after camp, and counts each corps member in the year of swearing-in only, although service runs across two calendar years. Rates are repeated with a two-year exposure window as a check. |
| A03 | Each geopolitical zone is assigned the states in the standard six-zone grouping, with the FCT in the North Central zone. | The zones have no statutory boundary list; the standard grouping is used in federal statistics and reporting. | `nysc_safety.coding.ZONES`; H1 | Only affects zone-level summaries; state-level results are unaffected. |
| A04 | A journey to camp, to the place of primary assignment or to passing-out is coded to the travel stage when the report places the person on a road, at a motor park or in a vehicle, and the report links the journey to NYSC service. | Reports rarely state the purpose of travel explicitly; the stage determines which policy controls apply. | `docs/coding_protocol.md` (lifecycle stage) | Journeys with no stated link to service are coded `personal_travel`; results for H2 are repeated with these journeys included. |
| A05 | Journeys that leave the orientation camp at the end of the course, including journeys to the home state before reporting at the place of primary assignment, are coded `travel_to_ppa`, and the travellers are coded `serving` because they have been sworn in. | Several reports describe sworn-in corps members abducted on the way home from camp; the protocol has no separate code for this leg, and it is closest to travel from camp to the posting. | `data/interim/incidents_working.csv`; `docs/coding_protocol.md` (lifecycle stage) | These journeys fall about three weeks after camp opening, outside the H2 windows around camp-opening and passing-out dates, so they count as incidents outside the windows. A version of H2 excluding them is reported as exploratory. |
| A06 | Harm to corps members on election duty as INEC ad hoc staff in their state of service is coded `ppa`. | Election duty takes place in the host community during the service year; the protocol has no separate code for it. | `data/interim/incidents_working.csv` | Election-duty incidents can be separated in exploratory analysis using the coding note. |
| A07 | Where a report gives only a weekday for the event ("on Sunday"), the event is dated to the most recent such weekday on or before the publication date, and `date_precision` is `day`. | Many reports name the weekday but not the date; the most recent such weekday is the reading consistent with the report's tense. | `data/interim/incidents_working.csv` | An error of one week is possible where a report is published several days after the event. The H2 test is repeated excluding incidents dated this way; they are identified in the coding note. |
| A08 | A journey by a serving corps member back to the place of primary assignment after an official activity, such as monthly clearance or final clearance, is coded `ppa`. | The journey is part of the duties of the service year and the protocol has no separate travel code for it. | `data/interim/incidents_working.csv` | Few incidents are affected; they are identified in the coding note. |
| A09 | Each state's share of corps members is constant from 2010 to 2026 and equals its median reported orientation-course size divided by the sum of median course sizes across states. | No series of corps members sworn in by state and year is published; news reports give course sizes for some batches in each state. Camp capacity, which limits course size, changes slowly. | `nysc_safety.exposure`; H3 and H4 | Shares are recomputed from sworn-in figures only, and from figures for 2019 onwards only; state rankings under each version are reported. States without any reported figure are excluded from rates and listed. |
| A10 | The national number of corps members in a year with no published total is interpolated linearly between the nearest years with a total, and held constant before the first and after the last such year. | National totals are published irregularly (NBS series to 2014, Director-General statements later). | `nysc_safety.exposure` | National totals scale every state equally, so state rankings (H3, H4) do not depend on them; only rate levels do. Interpolated years are flagged in `data/processed/exposure_by_state.csv`. |
| A11 | Where a date is missing from the NYSC calendar, camp closing is camp opening plus 20 days (and the reverse); a passing-out date reported for a batch without a stream applies to each stream of that batch; and a passing-out date still missing is camp closing plus the median interval between closing and passing-out among streams where both are reported (324 days). | Orientation courses last 21 days, and the service year runs about 11 months after camp; reported dates for many streams are incomplete. | `nysc_safety.camp_calendar`; H2 | Each date carries its basis (`reported`, `batch_report`, `derived`). H2 is repeated with reported dates only. Streams with no camp dates at all (2011 Batches A and C, 2013 Batch A, 2018 Batch B Stream II, 2021 Batch A Stream II and Batch B Stream I) contribute only the dates reported for them. |
| A12 | The population of each state for general kidnapping rates over 2010 to 2026 is its projected population in 2022, the latest year of the National Population Commission projection [S551]. | No official projection by state is published for any year after 2022, and no census has been held since 2006. A single year keeps the denominator the same for all states. | `data/interim/population_by_state.csv`; `nysc_safety.general_kidnapping`; H4 | States grew at different rates over 2007 to 2022 in S551, so a different reference year changes rates slightly. H4 uses ranks; rates are recomputed with the mean of the S551 populations for 2010 to 2022 and the rank correlation is reported. |
